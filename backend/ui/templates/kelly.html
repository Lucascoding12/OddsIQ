Kelly criterion

Finding an edge is half the job. The other half is how much to bet. Bet too little and your bankroll barely grows. Bet too much and a normal losing streak sets you back further than the edge can recover. Kelly is the bet size that grows a bankroll fastest over the long run.

The formula

bet = (p × d − 1) ÷ (d − 1)

p is the true chance the bet wins and d is the decimal odds you're getting. The top, p × d − 1, is your edge: what each dollar returns on average. The bottom, d − 1, is what each dollar wins. Big edge at short odds means a big bet; small edge at long odds means a small one. No edge means no bet.

Why almost nobody bets full Kelly

Kelly assumes you know p exactly. You never do. On OddsIQ, p comes from the sharp books, and they typically disagree with each other by more than a point. If your estimate is a little high, full Kelly is already over-betting. The curve below shows the cost: growth peaks at full Kelly, falls to zero around twice Kelly, and turns negative after. Half Kelly keeps about three quarters of the growth with a fraction of the swings, and quarter Kelly is where most professional bettors live.

Many bets at once

Kelly sizes one bet at a time. With several open together, the stakes add up fast, so the sheet at the bottom caps the total you have open and scales every bet down evenly to fit.
